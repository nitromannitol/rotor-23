# rotor-23

A machine-checked **Lean 4** formalization of the paper
[*Eulerian walkers on `ℤ²` have range exponent `2/3`*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23545)
(Ahmed Bou-Rabee and Yuval Peres, arXiv:2608.23545), built on
[`mathlib`](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4) and the
percolation library `PercolationContinuity` of
[`anthropics/formal-math`](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math).

[![CI](https://github.com/nitromannitol/rotor-23/actions/workflows/build.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/nitromannitol/rotor-23/actions/workflows/build.yml)
[![Comparator audit](https://github.com/nitromannitol/rotor-23/actions/workflows/comparator.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/nitromannitol/rotor-23/actions/workflows/comparator.yml)

## What this is

A rotor configuration assigns to each site of `ℤ²` one of the four directions
`N, E, S, W`, and the clockwise mechanism cycles them in that order.  A walker
at `X_t` turns the rotor at `X_t` one step clockwise and moves along it.  From
a fixed start `o`, `R_t = {X_0, …, X_t}` is the range, `T(n)` is the time at
which `n` circuits (each `deg(o)` successive returns to `o`) are complete, and
`A_n = R_{T(n)}`.  Theorem 1.1 of the paper says that when the initial rotors
are independent and uniform, then almost surely the walk is recurrent,
`n⁻¹ A_n` converges in Hausdorff distance to a deterministic compact convex set
`B` with the origin in its interior, `t^{-1/3} R_t` converges to `κ B` for a
deterministic `κ > 0`, and `|R_t| t^{-2/3}` converges to a deterministic
constant in `(0, ∞)`.  The theorem also covers doubly periodic plane graphs of
maximum degree three; Proposition 1.2 extends it to independent rotor laws
close to uniform in total variation, and Proposition 1.3 gives a doubly
periodic plane graph, the square lattice with `M` leaves attached to every
vertex, on which the walk is transient for large `M`.

This repository formalizes **every theorem, lemma and proposition of the
paper**: Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 1.2, each for the square lattice and for
doubly periodic graphs of maximum degree three, Proposition 1.3, and the
results of Sections 2 to 6.  Not formalized: the open questions of Section 7,
the figures, the simulations, and the survey of related work in Section 1.3.

**What is assumed.**  The paper quotes seven results from the literature
without proof.  Each is stated in Lean as a proposition in `Rotor/External/`,
and every theorem whose proof uses one takes it as an explicit hypothesis, so
the statement shows exactly which of them it rests on;
[`ASSUMPTIONS.md`](ASSUMPTIONS.md) lists the one still assumed, with its
verbatim Lean statement.  Six of the seven are proved in `Rotor/Bridge/`:
subcritical exponential decay for Bernoulli bond percolation on `ℤ²`, from the
percolation library; Kingman's subadditive ergodic theorem, from the shared
library `Lattice-Probability`; the abelian property of rotor-routing
(Holroyd–Levine–Mészáros–Peres–Propp–Wilson), by induction on legal routings;
Florescu–Levine–Peres' one-circuit property, by an injectivity argument
on the walk's traversed edges together with incoming/outgoing degree counts
at circuit times; Holroyd–Propp's Lemma 6 (a rotor walk that visits one
vertex infinitely often visits every vertex infinitely often), by propagating
infinitely-many-visits along a walk between any two vertices; and
Angel–Holroyd's theorem that recurrence does not depend on the starting
vertex, by the same propagation combined with the abelian property.  The
remaining one (Liggett–Schonmann–Stacey domination) is **not proved here**.
The main theorems are therefore conditional on it (Theorem 1.1 and
Proposition 1.2, on `LSS`); Proposition 1.3 is unconditional.

- **No `sorry`** anywhere in the library.  (Each Mathlib-only comparator
  challenge in `Audit/` contains its single intentional statement-level
  `sorry`, filled by the corresponding solution file.)
- **No custom `axiom`.**  The main theorems reduce to `mathlib`'s three
  standard foundational axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`,
  as [`Rotor/Meta/AxiomsAudit.lean`](Rotor/Meta/AxiomsAudit.lean) verifies.
  The cited results above are hypotheses, not axioms.
- Pinned to Lean `v4.32.0`, `mathlib` at revision
  `81a5d257c8e410db227a6665ed08f64fea08e997`, and `PercolationContinuity` at
  commit `795efb86`.

## Main results

The main theorems are stated in full in
[`Rotor/MainTheorems.lean`](Rotor/MainTheorems.lean), each proved by direct
application of its certified counterpart in `Rotor/Frozen/Main/`, so the
statements displayed there are byte-faithful to the certified ones.

* **`Rotor.main_square`** (Theorem 1.1, square lattice): on `ℤ²` with the
  clockwise mechanism and independent uniform initial rotors, there are a
  compact convex `B` with the origin in its interior and constants
  `κ, c > 0` such that almost surely the walk is recurrent, every circuit
  completes, `n⁻¹ A_n → B` and `t^{-1/3} R_t → κ B` in Hausdorff distance
  (coordinates centered at `o`), the inner and outer bounds
  `((1 - ε) n) • B` and `((1 + ε) n) • B` on `A_n` hold eventually, and
  `|R_t| / t^{2/3} → c`.  Hypothesis: Liggett–Schonmann–Stacey.
* **`Rotor.main_degree_three`** (Theorem 1.1, degree three): the same
  conclusions on a connected infinite graph of maximum degree three with a
  doubly periodic drawing `P : DoublyPeriodic G` and a doubly periodic
  mechanism, in the coordinates `P.emb`.  Same hypothesis.
* **`Rotor.perturbations_square`** (Proposition 1.2, square lattice): see
  below.  Same hypothesis.
* **`Rotor.perturbations_degree_three`** (Proposition 1.2, degree three): one
  `δ > 0` such that for independent rotor laws within total variation `δ` of
  uniform at every vertex the walk is almost surely recurrent from every
  start, and, when the marginals are invariant under the translation lattice,
  the conclusions of Theorem 1.1 hold.  Same hypothesis.
* **`Rotor.pendant_counterexample`** (Proposition 1.3): on the square lattice
  with `M ≥ 50331645 = 3 · 256³ - 3` leaves attached to every vertex and the
  clockwise order `N, E, S, W, L_1, …, L_M`, the walk with independent uniform
  initial rotors is almost surely not recurrent, from every start.
  Unconditional.

Proposition 1.2 on the square lattice gives one `δ > 0` for all independent
rotor laws whose marginal at every vertex has total variation distance less
than `δ` from uniform. The walk is almost surely recurrent from each start.
For every finite-index translation sublattice `Λ ≤ ℤ²`, invariance of the
marginal laws under `Λ` also gives a deterministic compact convex body `B`
with the origin in its interior and positive constants `κ, c`. In coordinates
centered at the starting vertex,
`n^(-1) A_n → B`, `t^(-1/3) R_t → κ B`, and `|R_t| / t^(2/3) → c` almost surely.
The radius is independent of `Λ`. The frozen statement also includes finite
circuit times and the inner and outer lattice-point bounds.

See [`Rotor.Frozen.perturbations_square`](Rotor/Frozen/Main/PerturbSquare.lean); its cited inputs are listed below.

## Scope and faithfulness

Every statement of the paper is registered: one Lean declaration per
theorem, lemma or proposition (Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 1.2 as two each),
and one proposition per cited result.  A registered statement's text between
the lines `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-END` is pinned
by the SHA-256 of those bytes in [`ledger/manifest.yaml`](ledger/manifest.yaml),
together with the paper `\label` and line range it transcribes; the proof
after the end marker may be rewritten freely.  In the manifest, a registered
statement is `SEALED` when it is proved with an axiom closure of exactly the
three standard axioms, and `FROZEN` when it is a cited result assumed rather
than proved.

The paper-to-Lean map, node by node, is
[`CORRESPONDENCE.md`](CORRESPONDENCE.md), which also records the three places
where a Lean statement is not literally the paper's: a routing is its initial
state and its list of actuated vertices; `prop:passage` is conjoined with
`T(n) < ⊤`; `prop:live-recurrence` is asserted for the fixed start `o`.
Modelling decisions:

- A graph is a Mathlib `SimpleGraph`, locally finite; a directed edge out of
  `v` is an element of `G.neighborSet v`; a rotor mechanism is a family of
  cyclic permutations of the neighbor sets (`Rotor/Model.lean`).  The square
  lattice is `ℤ × ℤ` with the directions numbered `0 = N, 1 = E, 2 = S,
  3 = W`, as an instance of the general model (`Rotor/Square.lean`).
- `T n` takes values in `ℕ∞`, with `⊤` when no such time exists (the paper's
  `inf ∅ = ∞`).  `A n` is defined through `(T n).toNat`, so at `T n = ⊤` it is
  the value `R 0 = {o}`; every statement about `A n` carries `T n < ⊤`, or
  almost-sure recurrence.
- In Theorem 1.1, `B`, `κ` and the limit constant are bound before the
  almost-sure quantifier, so they are deterministic.  Conclusion (ii) is stated
  both as Hausdorff convergence and as the inner and outer bounds
  `((1 - ε) n) • B` and `((1 + ε) n) • B` on `A n`.
- The law of the initial rotors is a product law, Mathlib's
  `Measure.infinitePi` over the neighbor sets with their discrete σ-algebra;
  almost-sure statements are `∀ᵐ ρ ∂μ`.  The total variation distance between
  two laws on a finite set is `sup_s |μ s - ν s|` over all subsets.
- A doubly periodic graph is the data `P : DoublyPeriodic G`: an injective
  embedding `V → ℝ²`, a `ℤ²`-action by graph automorphisms with a basis of the
  translation lattice, and a choice of orbit representatives.  Connectedness
  and infiniteness of `V` are hypotheses of the statements, not fields of `P`.
- Proposition 1.3 is stated with the explicit `M₀ = 50331645` and asserts
  almost-sure non-recurrence from every start vertex.

Every statement that transcribes a paper statement has a recorded
clause-by-clause reading against it (`tools/check_clauses.py`), its
quantifier order is checked mechanically (`tools/check_constants.py`), and its
exponents are matched against the paper's (`tools/check_exponents.py`).

## Verified against a Mathlib-only statement

So that the main claims can be read without trusting the 31,000-line
development, all five main theorems are restated using **only Mathlib**, no
project definitions, in `Audit/<Thm>/Challenge.lean`.  Each challenge rebuilds
the model from Mathlib primitives (the rotor walk on a locally finite graph,
its range, circuit times and circuit ranges, recurrence, particle routings,
the product law of the initial rotors, total variation, doubly periodic
graphs, the square lattice with its clockwise mechanism, the graph `G_M`, and
the cited results it assumes) and contains one intentional statement-level
`sorry`, which the corresponding `Solution.lean` fills from the library
through the bridges in `Audit/Support/`.  The configurations in
`Audit/*/comparator.json` are for
[`leanprover/comparator`](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator), which
confirms that the two statements have identical elaborated types and that
the proof reduces to the three standard axioms (see
[`Audit/README.md`](Audit/README.md)).

The five pairs are `MainSquare`, `MainDegreeThree`, `PerturbationsSquare`,
`PerturbationsDegreeThree` and `PendantCounterexample`.  All five solutions
build and depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and
`Audit/StatementRegression.lean` checks locally that each solution statement is
exactly the challenge statement and mentions no repository constant.  The
comparator was run on all five pairs on 2026-09-27, at commit `f5c29c7`, and
every pair passed with the Lean kernel and with the independent nanoda
kernel; results and reproduction steps are in
[`Audit/COMPARATOR_RUNS.md`](Audit/COMPARATOR_RUNS.md).  The workflow
[`.github/workflows/comparator.yml`](.github/workflows/comparator.yml) runs it
on request.

## Building

The project uses [`elan`](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) (the Lean
toolchain manager) and Lake.  The toolchain is pinned in
[`lean-toolchain`](lean-toolchain) (`leanprover/lean4:v4.32.0`), so `elan`
installs the right Lean version automatically.

```bash
# from the repository root, the first time
./bootstrap.sh       # fetch the pinned percolation dependency, lake exe cache get, lake build
# afterwards
lake build           # compile the project
```

`bootstrap.sh` is needed because `lake update` cannot check out the
percolation dependency by itself: the pinned commit `795efb86` of
`anthropics/formal-math` is not reachable from any branch tip of that
repository, so Lake's clone does not contain it and the checkout fails with
`fatal: reference is not a tree`.  The script fetches that object by hash and
then runs `lake exe cache get` and `lake build`.  `lake exe cache get`
requires the committed [`lake-manifest.json`](lake-manifest.json), which pins
the exact dependency revisions.  The default target `Rotor` is about 8,850
build jobs, nearly all of them Mathlib's, which the cache supplies.

```bash
lake build Rotor.Meta.AxiomsAudit   # print the axioms of the five main theorems
lake build Audit                    # the comparator challenges and solutions
lake build Audit.StatementRegression
```

To use the library, `import Rotor` pulls in the whole development; the main
results are in `import Rotor.MainTheorems`.

The checkers in `tools/` need Python 3 and PyYAML (`pip install pyyaml`).

| command | what it guarantees |
|---|---|
| `python3 tools/check_axioms.py` | runs Lean's `#print axioms` on every node's export and reports any node whose closure contains `sorryAx`; `--emit` also rewrites each theorem node's `state:` in the manifest from the result |
| `python3 tools/check_manifest.py` | each frozen statement's bytes match its recorded SHA-256, every file under `Rotor/Frozen/` belongs to exactly one node, and no `axiom`, `admit` or `sorryAx` token, and no unregistered `sorry`, occurs under `Rotor/` |
| `python3 tools/check_warnings.py` | the build of `Rotor` emits no warning other than the `sorry` warnings the manifest authorizes (it authorizes none) |
| `python3 tools/check_coverage.py` | every theorem, lemma, proposition and corollary environment in `paper/rotor.tex` is claimed by a manifest node; the paper's open problems are excluded |
| `python3 tools/check_clauses.py` | every node that transcribes a paper statement has a recorded clause-by-clause reading against it |
| `python3 tools/check_constants.py` | in each frozen statement, an existential constant is bound before the variables the paper says it may not depend on |
| `python3 tools/check_exponents.py` | every exponent in a paper statement appears in its Lean statement, or the difference is recorded with its reason |

Also in `tools/`: `paper_anchors.py` checks that the line ranges in the
manifest match the paper's `\label`s (`--fix` repairs them);
`paper_citations.py` does the same for the `rotor.tex:<a>-<b>` citations in
Lean docstrings; `sync_docs.py` regenerates the marked blocks of this file and
of `CORRESPONDENCE.md` from the manifest (`--write` applies, the bare command
checks); `assumptions.py` regenerates `ASSUMPTIONS.md`; `certificate.py`
regenerates `CERTIFICATE.md` (`--check` compares it with the file on disk);
`freeze.py` registers or refreshes a node in the manifest.

## Repository layout

```
Rotor/
  MainTheorems.lean   the main theorems, stated in full
  Frozen/             the certified statement surface, one frozen statement per file:
                      Main/, Circuits/, Shape/, DegreeThree/, Square/
  External/           the seven cited results, each a Prop (two still frozen)
  Bridge/             External.SubcriticalDecay, External.Kingman, External.Abelian,
                      External.OneCircuit and External.VisitsAllOfVisitsOne proved
                      from the percolation library, the shared library
                      Lattice-Probability, by induction on legal routings, by an
                      injectivity/degree-count argument, and by walk propagation
                      respectively, and the statements that use them with each
                      discharged
  Support/            the lemmas and constructions the frozen statements are proved from
  Meta/               AxiomsAudit.lean
  Basic.lean          the square-lattice model: sites, directions, the step rule
  Model.lean          the model on a general locally finite graph
  Law.lean            the law of the initial rotors: product laws, the uniform law
  Routing.lean, Traversal.lean   particle routings, traversals and departures
  Square.lean, Periodic.lean, Pendant.lean, Percolation.lean, Dual.lean,
  Events.lean, Exploration.lean  the square lattice, doubly periodic graphs,
                      the graph G_M, Bernoulli bond percolation, the dual lattice,
                      the events and the exploration of Section 5
Rotor.lean            the root module (imports the whole library)
Audit/                Mathlib-only comparator challenges and solutions
ASSUMPTIONS.md        the cited results assumed, with their Lean statements (generated)
CORRESPONDENCE.md     paper ↔ Lean, node by node
CERTIFICATE.md        generated record of the toolchain, the build, each node's
                      axiom closure and the SHA-256 of each frozen statement
ledger/manifest.yaml  one row per registered statement: hash, paper label, state
paper/rotor.tex       the paper, pinned by the SHA-256 in ledger/manifest.yaml
tools/                the checkers and generators listed under Building
bootstrap.sh          first-time setup (the percolation dependency)
```

## How this was built

The Lean code in this repository was written by AI models under the
supervision of the author.  Nearly all of it was written by Claude (Fable 5.1,
then Opus 5) in Claude Code, which also supervised the other agents: Fable 5.1
wrote the statements and the proofs through the seal of Lemma 5.3, and Opus 5
proved subcritical decay from the percolation library and prepared the
repository.  OpenAI's gpt-6-astra, through Codex, reviewed proofs and proposed
simplifications that were applied, and wrote the integrability hypothesis of
the Kingman input and Proposition 1.2 on the square lattice under finite-index
translation subgroups.  GLM-5.3 audited statements and proofs, and
GLM-5.3-flash and DeepSeek-v4.1-flash drafted compile-filtered proof
candidates for short goals, a few of which were accepted after checking.
Claude Sonnet 5 rewrote this README, and Claude Opus 5.5 wrote
`Rotor/MainTheorems.lean`, the comparator surface in `Audit/`, and the release
documentation.  In a final pass on 2026-09-26 and 2026-09-27, a Claude Opus 5.5
general in Claude Code and Claude Sonnet 5 subagents it dispatched proved
further cited inputs and removed them as hypotheses of the main theorems,
brought the comparator surface in line with the certified statements and re-ran
the comparator, and named and documented the remaining helper lemmas; OpenAI's
gpt-6-luna and gpt-5.6-luna, through Codex, were commissioned for some of those
proofs, and everything kept was checked and gated.  The models, tooling, cost,
and review status are disclosed in
full in [`formalization.yaml`](formalization.yaml), following the
[mathlib-initiative](https://github.com/mathlib-initiative/formalization.yaml)
standard.

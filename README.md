# rotor-23

A Lean 4 formalization of

> **Eulerian walkers on `ℤ²` have range exponent `2/3`**
> Ahmed Bou-Rabee and Yuval Peres, [arXiv:2608.23545](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23545)

Every theorem, lemma and proposition of the paper is stated and proved in Lean 4
over Mathlib: Theorem 1.1 for the square lattice and for doubly periodic graphs
of maximum degree three, Propositions 1.2 and 1.3, and the results of Sections 2
to 6.  The development rests on Mathlib at a pinned revision and on seven
results the paper cites without proof; each is a `Prop` in `Rotor/External/`
that the theorems using it take as an explicit hypothesis.  The percolation
library `PercolationContinuity` of `anthropics/formal-math` is a Lake dependency
used by `Rotor/Bridge/`.

## The model

A rotor configuration assigns to each site of `ℤ²` one of the four directions
`N, E, S, W`, and the clockwise mechanism cycles them in that order.  A walker at
`X_t` turns the rotor at `X_t` one step clockwise and moves along it.  From a
fixed start `o`, `R_t = {X_0, …, X_t}` is the range, `T(n)` is the time at which
`n` circuits (each `deg(o)` successive returns to `o`) are complete, and
`A_n = R_{T(n)}`.  Theorem 1.1 says that when the initial rotors are independent
and uniform, then almost surely the walk is recurrent (it visits every site
infinitely often), `n⁻¹ A_n` converges in Hausdorff distance to a deterministic
compact convex set `B` with the origin in its interior, `t^{-1/3} R_t` converges
to `κ B` for a deterministic `κ > 0`, and `|R_t| t^{-2/3}` converges to a
deterministic constant in `(0, ∞)`.  The theorem also covers doubly periodic
plane graphs of maximum degree three; Proposition 1.2 extends it to independent
rotor laws close to uniform in total variation, and Proposition 1.3 gives a
doubly periodic plane graph, the square lattice with `M` leaves attached to
every vertex, on which the walk is transient for large `M`.

## Terms

These words are used throughout with fixed meanings.

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **registered statement**, also called a **node** | one Lean declaration transcribing one statement of the paper, or one result the paper cites. Each has an entry in `ledger/manifest.yaml`. |
| **frozen statement** | the declaration's text between the lines `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-END`, pinned by the SHA-256 of those bytes. It cannot change without the change being recorded in the manifest. The proof after the end marker may be rewritten freely. |
| **`SEALED`** | the node is **proved** here: a complete proof, whose axiom closure contains only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. |
| **`FROZEN`** | the node is **assumed**, not proved: a result the paper quotes from the literature, stated as a proposition in `Rotor/External/` and carried as an explicit hypothesis by every theorem whose proof uses it. Every one is listed with its statement in [`ASSUMPTIONS.md`](ASSUMPTIONS.md). |
| **`DRAFT_SORRY`** | the statement is pinned but its proof is still open, registered as a `sorry`. |
| **cited input**, **external** | a `FROZEN` node, in the sense above. |
| **axiom closure** | the axioms a proof ultimately rests on, as reported by Lean's `#print axioms`. |
| **`sorryAx`** | the axiom Lean inserts for an unproved `sorry`; its presence in an axiom closure means the result is not proved. |

The word *frozen* carries two senses, so it is worth separating them: every
registered statement is frozen in the sense of being **pinned by hash**, while
the state `FROZEN` marks the subset that is **assumed rather than proved**.

## How a statement is tied to the paper

Each theorem in `Rotor/Frozen/` sits between `-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-BEGIN` and
`-- FROZEN-STATEMENT-END`; each cited input in `Rotor/External/` sits between
the same markers.  Those bytes are the contract: `ledger/manifest.yaml`
records their SHA-256 and the paper `\label` they transcribe (with the line
range in `paper/rotor.tex`; for a cited input, the external source).  The proof
follows the end marker and may be rewritten freely.  `tools/check_manifest.py`
recomputes each hash.  Every frozen statement that transcribes a paper statement
is read against the paper clause by clause, and the reading is recorded
(`tools/check_clauses.py`); the order of its quantifiers is checked
mechanically (`tools/check_constants.py`); its exponents are matched against the
paper's (`tools/check_exponents.py`).

## Building and checking

The Lean toolchain is pinned in `lean-toolchain` (`leanprover/lean4:v4.32.0`)
and Mathlib in `lake-manifest.json` (revision
`81a5d257c8e410db227a6665ed08f64fea08e997`).

```
elan toolchain install $(cat lean-toolchain)
./bootstrap.sh          # first time: fetch the pinned percolation dependency, then lake exe cache get and lake build
lake build              # afterwards
```

`bootstrap.sh` is needed because `lake update` cannot check out the percolation dependency by
itself: the pinned commit `795efb86` of `anthropics/formal-math` is not reachable from any branch
tip of that repository, so Lake's clone does not contain it and the checkout fails with `fatal:
reference is not a tree`.  The script fetches that object by hash and then runs `lake exe cache
get` and `lake build`.

The checkers need Python 3 and PyYAML (`pip install pyyaml`).

| command | what it guarantees |
|---|---|

| `python3 tools/check_manifest.py` | each frozen statement's bytes match its recorded SHA-256, every file under `Rotor/Frozen/` belongs to exactly one node, and no `axiom`, `admit` or `sorryAx` token, and no unregistered `sorry`, occurs under `Rotor/` |

## Scope

Formalized: every theorem, lemma and proposition of the paper.  Theorem 1.1 and
Proposition 1.2 are each formalized as two nodes, one for the square lattice and
one for doubly periodic graphs of maximum degree three.  The seven cited results
are not proved here, except that `External.SubcriticalDecay` is derived from the
percolation library in `Rotor/Bridge/`.

Not formalized: the open questions of Section 7, the figures, the simulations,
and the survey of related work in Section 1.3.

Modelling decisions:

- `Rotor/Basic.lean` models the square lattice only: sites are `ℤ × ℤ`, the
  directions are `Fin 4` numbered `0 = N, 1 = E, 2 = S, 3 = W`, and the
  clockwise mechanism is `+ 1`.  The frozen statements use the general model of
  `Rotor/Model.lean`, of which `Rotor/Square.lean` gives the square lattice as
  an instance.
- `T n` takes values in `ℕ∞`, with `⊤` when no such time exists (the paper's
  `inf ∅ = ∞`).  `A n` is defined through `(T n).toNat`, so at `T n = ⊤` it is
  the value `R 0 = {o}`; every statement about `A n` carries `T n < ⊤`, or
  almost-sure recurrence.
- In Theorem 1.1, `B`, `κ` and the limit constant are bound before the
  almost-sure quantifier, so they are deterministic.  Conclusion (ii) is stated
  both as Hausdorff convergence and as the inner and outer bounds
  `((1 - ε) n) • B` and `((1 + ε) n) • B` on `A n`.
- The law of the initial rotors is a product law, Mathlib's
  `Measure.infinitePi` over the neighbor sets with their discrete σ-algebra;
  almost-sure statements are `∀ᵐ ρ ∂μ`.  The total variation distance between
  two laws on a finite set is `sup_s |μ s - ν s|` over all subsets.
- A doubly periodic graph is the data `P : DoublyPeriodic G`: an injective
  embedding `V → ℝ²`, a `ℤ²`-action by graph automorphisms with a basis of the
  translation lattice, and a choice of orbit representatives.  Connectedness and
  infiniteness of `V` are hypotheses of the statements, not fields of `P`.
- Proposition 1.3 is stated with the explicit `M₀ = 50331645` (`3 · 256³ - 3`)
  and asserts almost-sure non-recurrence from every start vertex.
- `CORRESPONDENCE.md` lists where a Lean statement is not literally the paper's:
  a routing is its initial state and its list of actuated vertices;
  `prop:passage` is conjoined with `T(n) < ⊤`; `prop:live-recurrence` is
  asserted for the fixed start `o`.

## Small perturbations on the square lattice

<!-- SQUARE-PERTURBATIONS-BEGIN (generated by tools/sync_docs.py) -->

Proposition 1.2 on the square lattice gives one `δ > 0` for all independent
rotor laws whose marginal at every vertex has total variation distance less
than `δ` from uniform. The walk is almost surely recurrent from each start.
For every finite-index translation sublattice `Λ ≤ ℤ²`, invariance of the
marginal laws under `Λ` also gives a deterministic compact convex body `B`
with the origin in its interior and positive constants `κ, c`. In coordinates
centered at the starting vertex,
`n^(-1) A_n → B`, `t^(-1/3) R_t → κ B`, and `|R_t| / t^(2/3) → c` almost surely.
The radius is independent of `Λ`. The frozen statement also includes finite
circuit times and the inner and outer lattice-point bounds.

See [`Rotor.Frozen.perturbations_square`](Rotor/Frozen/Main/PerturbSquare.lean); its cited inputs are listed below.
